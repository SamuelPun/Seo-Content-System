The article is not about correcting the reader — it is about giving them clarity. The 183-day rule appears in two distinct places (Dutch domestic tax residency determination and the Netherlands–HK double tax arrangement), and most articles conflate them or leave out the practical steps. This guide separates them clearly, explains both, and walks through the emigration mechanics that make the exit stick.

## Structure

### Section 1: Introduction (~150 words)
Frame the reader's situation: planning the move, doing the research, want to know exactly what the 183-day rule means for their Dutch taxes. Establish what this guide covers: what the rule is, where it applies, and what steps properly close Dutch tax residency. No throat-clearing. No definition in sentence one.

### Section 2: What "Dutch tax resident" actually means (~200 words)
The Belastingdienst (Dutch Tax and Customs Administration) taxes Dutch residents on worldwide income — Box 1 (employment/business), Box 2 (substantial shareholdings), Box 3 (savings and investments). The key point: tax residency is determined by the totality of your ties to the Netherlands, not simply by where you are. Family, home, bank accounts, business interests, social life — all count. Days are one signal, not the only one.

Explain Box 1/2/3 on first use. Explain "Belastingdienst" on first use.

### Section 3: Where the 183-day rule appears — and what it actually triggers (~250 words)
The rule shows up in two distinct places:

**Place 1 — The Netherlands–HK Arrangement for the Avoidance of Double Taxation.**
Article 15 of the arrangement: if you work in HK for a Dutch employer and spend more than 183 days in HK within a 12-month period, HK gets primary taxing rights over that employment income. Under 183 days, the Netherlands may still tax it.

**Place 2 — Dutch domestic residency determination.**
Spending more than 183 days outside the Netherlands in a calendar year is used by the Belastingdienst as a supporting signal that you have left. It does not automatically end your Dutch residency — you need the formal exit steps too — but it strengthens your position significantly.

Include a worked numeric example: Dutch employee, annual salary €120,000, moves to HK in July (mid-year). Show what portion is taxed where, before and after the 183-day threshold is crossed.

### Section 4: The Dutch emigration checklist — how to actually close your tax residency (~300 words)
Four concrete steps:

1. **De-register from the BRP** (Basisregistratie Personen — the Dutch municipal population register) at your local gemeente before you leave. This is the administrative signal that you have left the Netherlands. Explain BRP on first use.
2. **File the M-form** (M-biljet — the departure-year tax return). This is the formal Belastingdienst declaration that you stopped being a Dutch resident partway through the year. Explain M-form on first use.
3. **Sever Dutch ties that keep the residency claim alive.** Practical list: sell or formally transfer Dutch property, close or restructure Dutch bank accounts, cancel Dutch-registered vehicles, review regular return visits (frequency matters). The Belastingdienst looks at the full picture.
4. **Review Dutch pension and investment accounts.** Some Dutch financial products have specific treatment on emigration — this is worth checking with a cross-border tax adviser before you go.

Close the section with a plain statement: the Belastingdienst can and does challenge non-residency claims, especially when Dutch ties remain active. De-registering from the BRP is necessary but not sufficient on its own.

### Section 5: What happens if the exit is incomplete (~200 words)
If the Belastingdienst rules you never fully left Dutch tax residency, you face dual-residency status — filing obligations in both countries and potential back-tax claims on your HK income. The Netherlands–HK arrangement provides double taxation relief, but it does not eliminate the filing burden or the risk of penalties for non-disclosure. Short scenario: Dutch expat in HK for three years, Belastingdienst audits and finds active Dutch rental income, Dutch home retained, regular visits — outcome and cost.

Do not catastrophise; this section is informational, not alarming. End with: the exit process is straightforward when done properly.

### Section 6: What you're landing into — HK tax in 30 seconds (~200 words)
Contrast paragraph. Hong Kong uses territorial taxation: only income sourced in Hong Kong is taxed. No tax on foreign income. No capital gains tax. No equivalent of Box 3 wealth tax. Salaries tax is capped at 15% (standard rate) or 17% (marginal rate on the first HK$5 million). For most Dutch relocators, the effective HK tax burden is significantly lower than the Dutch equivalent.

This section is the payoff — the contrast that makes the Dutch exit worth doing carefully. Keep it punchy, not promotional.

|------|------------------------|
| Belastingdienst | Dutch Tax and Customs Administration |
| BRP | Basisregistratie Personen — Dutch municipal population register |
| M-form (M-biljet) | Departure-year tax return filed with the Belastingdienst |
